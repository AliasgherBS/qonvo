01

How deep this went

This was not a click-through. The rule was: if a check can be made true or false by doing the thing, do the thing. Everything below was performed against the live tenant test01 on a paid Starter plan, signed in as the owner, and separately against the admin console as admin@qonvo.dev.

What was actually done, not just looked at

  • Files really ingested. A CSV, a DOCX, a hand-built minimal PDF and a genuine 391 KB PDF were uploaded and polled to completion. Two of the four failed, in two different ways.
  • Writes really written. A knowledge gap was answered through the UI and became a live source. A teammate was invited, the seat cap was hit, the invitation was revoked. Config was pushed past its character cap and the rejection was verified against the stored value.
  • A tenant was created and destroyed. "QA Throwaway Salon" was created from the admin console, suspended, reactivated, marked paid, given a trial end, had its owner password reset, was impersonated, and was then deleted through the typed-name confirmation.
  • Real customer messages. Five WhatsApp messages sent from a real phone to the live number, including a voice note and a three-message burst, then traced through the inbox, the transcript, the skill counters, the gaps log and the analytics.
  • Measured, not eyeballed. Element positions read from the rendered document, plan entitlements read back from the API rather than from the screen, worker stack traces read from container logs, and JWT claims base64-decoded and inspected.

Deliberately not done

  • Nothing outbound to a stranger. No manual reply was sent from the inbox, because it would leave the system as a real WhatsApp message. Awaiting a yes.
  • The live session was not touched. Fleet's Stop and Logout would disconnect a working number and force a QR rescan. Read only.
  • No checkout was completed. Upgrading would take a real payment.
  • The two real customer tenants were not touched. All lifecycle work went through the throwaway.

Confirmed by doing, this round

  • Prompt caps enforce exactly as specified. A 2,500-character instruction returned 422 with "Custom instructions is limited to 2,000 characters. This is 2,500." and the stored value was unchanged at 1,821.
  • Seat entitlement holds. Invite returned 201; the second invite returned 402 with "No seats left on your plan. Upgrade to invite more teammates."; revoke returned 204 and the count went back to zero.
  • Debounce works. Three messages sent in one burst arrived as three inbound rows with an identical timestamp and produced exactly one reply, which referenced both of them.
  • Speech-to-text is excellent. A spoken English voice note transcribed to "Hi, hope you're doing well. I just wanted to know that what kind of services you guys offer and what are the range of the prices that you guys cost?" — clean, unedited.
  • Handoff fires and is visible. The unanswerable question escalated, set the conversation to needs human, raised a notification naming the customer with an Open chat link, and logged a knowledge gap.
  • Impersonation is properly bounded. The minted token carries act: {sub: "admin@qonvo.dev"} and qonvo_admin: false, reads its own tenant, and answers 403 on admin routes.
  • Tenant delete is careful. It requires the business name typed exactly, then purges and redirects.
  • Nothing in the console. Zero JavaScript errors and zero failed requests across every page, at four widths, in both themes.
03

What is broken

F1
Critical

A real PDF is stuck on "Processing" forever, and the worker dies silently.

A genuine 391 KB PDF was uploaded. It never left pending_ingest. The worker log says why:

sqlalchemy.exc.DBAPIError: (asyncpg.exceptions.CharacterNotInRepertoireError)
invalid byte sequence for encoding "UTF8": 0x00
  ... sqlalchemy/orm/persistence.py, in _emit_insert_statements
  ... engine/base.py, in _exec_insertmany_context

Text extracted from the PDF contains NUL bytes, which a Postgres text column cannot hold. The bulk insert of knowledge chunks dies, the job dies with it, and nothing marks the source failed. The owner sees "Processing" indefinitely, with no error, no retry and no timeout. This will hit any layout-heavy PDF, which is most price lists, menus and service directories.

FixStrip \x00 from extracted text before storage, and give the ingest job a failure path that writes error plus a reason. A job that can crash must not be able to leave a row claiming to be in progress.

F2
Critical

Two customer messages were processed and never answered.

qonvo_messages_processed_total{direction="inbound"} = 39
qonvo_replies_sent_total                            = 34
qonvo_pipeline_gate_total{gate="paused"}            =  3
qonvo_job_failures_total{function="process_conversation"} = 2
qonvo_provider_errors_total{kind="llm"}             =  6

Thirty-nine in, thirty-four out. Three were correctly withheld by the paused gate. The other two crashed the pipeline. One of them is identifiable in the transcript: a voice note of 5 September at 23:07:59, transcribed correctly to "Please let me know that what services do you have from L?", with no outbound message after it, ever. Six LLM provider errors sit behind the same counter.

A customer message that disappears is the worst failure this product has, because nobody learns about it. The owner sees a chat with no reply and assumes the customer went quiet.

FixA crashed turn must retry, and after the last retry must leave something behind: a notification, a flag on the conversation, or a fallback reply. Silence is not an acceptable terminal state.

F3
High

Marking a tenant paid in the admin console leaves them on trial limits.

The throwaway tenant was set to plan: paid. Reading entitlements back:

test01             | plan: paid | messages allowed: 1000
QA Throwaway Salon | plan: paid | messages allowed:  300   ← trial quota

The cause is visible in the control itself: the button is a binary toggle, Mark as paid against Start 14-day trial. It sets a plan label, not a plan key, so apply_plan never runs and no entitlements are derived from the catalogue. The billing design's stated invariant is that "a plan change can never leave a stale quota"; the manual path, which exists precisely for taking payment outside a gateway, breaks it.

Consequence: take a bank transfer, mark the customer paid, and they hit a wall at 300 messages while the console says they are on a paid plan.

FixMake the control a plan picker over the real catalogue keys and route it through apply_plan. The PUT /api/admin/tenants/{id}/subscription endpoint that does this correctly already exists and has no interface.

F4
High

A customer's WhatsApp session has been dead and nobody was told.

Fleet Health shows Husain bh Ezzy at FAILED. The page reports it accurately. Nothing else does: no notification to that owner, no email, no alert to you, and no badge anywhere in their own dashboard. Their rep has simply stopped answering, and the only way to know is for somebody to open the admin console and look.

FixThe scheduler already scans the fleet. A session that leaves WORKING should raise an owner notification and an email within minutes, and show a banner in that tenant's dashboard.

F5
Medium

An ingestion failure shows no reason and offers no retry.

The second PDF failed cleanly and the table reads "Nothing indexed · Error". The API carries no error field at all. The owner cannot tell whether the file was corrupt, too large, an unsupported variant, or whether the system had a bad minute. There is no way to try again short of deleting and re-uploading.

FixPersist the failure reason, show it in the row, and put a Retry next to it.

F6
Medium

The notification badge never clears, and the voice notice is both wrong and duplicated.

Opening the panel marks nothing read. There is no per-item control. POST /api/notifications/{id}/read exists and is never called, so the badge can only ever count up.

Two of the three notifications say "Your rep has used its 5 voice minutes for this month", one at six hours ago and one at seven. The spec requires that notice once per period. Meanwhile the meter reads 2 min of 5, so voice was never exhausted at all, and voice replies have kept working. The notice survived the metering correction with nothing to reconcile it.

FixMark read on open. Dedupe the notice on the period, not the day. And when a meter is corrected downward, retract or supersede any notice it invalidated.

F7
Medium

Three different voice numbers in three places.

Owner billing page   :   2 min of 5     (120 s)
Admin usage endpoint :  89 voice_seconds
System health        : 1069 voice_seconds (platform)

The middle two should agree for a single-voice-user platform and do not. The limits spec asked for one shared computation for exactly this reason. Whatever else is true, an owner and an operator looking at the same tenant should not read different numbers.

FixOne function, called from all three surfaces, with the platform counter clearly labelled as a different scope.

F8
Low

"Last indexed: Never" on sources that are indexed.

Both live sources are Ready with real passage counts and both show Never. The last_ingested field is null in the API too, so it is not written on a successful ingest.

F9
Low

"Hi" is the top knowledge gap, and the analytics tile now advertises it.

The gap detector counts greetings as retrieval misses. Hi ×2 currently leads the list, above the refund complaint. It also leads the "Top questions the bot couldn't answer" tile, which now carries an Answer these in Knowledge call to action pointing at it. The most valuable feedback loop in the product is being polluted by hello.

FixDo not log a gap for a turn with no informational intent. A short greeting, a thanks or a goodbye is not a question the knowledge failed to answer.

F10
Low

Smaller things worth a line each.

Analytics skips empty days. The axis reads Sep 5, Sep 6, Sep 8 as three evenly spaced bars, so a gap looks like continuity.

Uploads are one file at a time, and the picker accepts only .pdf .docx .csv although plain text and markdown are supported by the parser.

Validation errors echo the whole rejected input back. A 2,500-character rejection returns all 2,500 characters in the response body. For payment_details that means account numbers in error surfaces and logs.

Login had no maintenance state. During a redeploy the dashboard threw CredentialsSignin while the API answered 200 for the same credentials, and the form told the user to check their password. A build and its API should not be able to disagree silently.

04

The conversation engine

This is the part that matters most, because it is the product. Three separate problems are tangled together here, and only one of them is a bug.

The prompt, as actually assembled

┌─ SYSTEM  (position 0, byte-stable, prompt-cached) ──────────────┐
│ You are the AI customer representative for test01.              │  ① business_name
│                                                                 │  ② persona — EMPTY here
│ Tone: Warm, Concise, Direct.                                    │  ③ tone
│ <your 1,821-character custom_instructions>                      │  ④ the bulk of the prompt
│                                                                 │
│ Answer ONLY using the business knowledge provided below. If     │  ⑤ GROUNDING_INSTRUCTION
│ the answer is not covered, say you'll connect them with the        (hardcoded, pipeline.py:71)
│ team and call the human_handoff tool — never invent facts,
│ prices, policies, or availability.
│ Reply in the same language AND the same script the customer     │  ⑥ language rule
│ used. If they write Urdu in Roman letters, reply in Roman…         (from reply_language setting)
│ If the customer's language is genuinely unclear, use en.        │  ⑦ primary_language
│ Keep replies concise and conversational, WhatsApp style…        │  ⑧ format
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
┌─ HISTORY  (append-only window, last N turns) ───────────────────┐
│ ~40 prior turns. In this conversation roughly 70% are Urdu      │  ⑨ the problem
│ or Roman Urdu.                                                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
┌─ FINAL USER TURN  (build_turn_prompt) ──────────────────────────┐
│ Business knowledge:                                             │  ⑩ RAG, capped ~2k tokens
│   <retrieved chunks>                                            │
│ Summary of the conversation so far:                             │  ⑪ rolling summary
│   <compressed older turns>                                      │     also Urdu-flavoured
│ [This issue has already been passed to the team…]               │  ⑫ only when needs_human
│ Customer message:                                               │  ⑬ the actual words, LAST
│   <text, or the STT transcript for a voice note>                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Two structural facts follow from that diagram. The language rule at ⑥ is separated from the message it governs at ⑬ by the entire history and the entire summary. And ④, your own instructions, is by volume the largest single input to every reply — larger than the grounding rule, the language rule and the format rule combined.

E1
Critical

English in, Roman Urdu out — and nothing in the system detects the language of the message it is answering.

The evidence is unambiguous in the transcript:

06:13:37  IN   "Hello / What services do you offer?"        EN
06:13:38  OUT  "Hello! We offer a range of salon and…"      EN   ✓
06:13:57  IN   "Do you provide laser tattoo removal?"       EN
06:13:57  OUT  "Assalamualaikum! Laser tattoo removal ka…"  UR   ✗
06:14:29  IN   "Book me a facial tomorrow"                  EN
06:14:29  OUT  "Ji bilkul. Facial tomorrow ke liye…"        UR   ✗
06:15:42  IN   [voice note, STT: clean English]             EN
06:15:42  OUT  "Assalamualaikum! Jee bilkul—Depilex mein…"  UR   ✗

It held for exactly one turn, then the history reasserted itself. The voice case rules out the audio path: the transcript was flawless English and the reply was Roman Urdu, so this is not detection failing on speech, it is the same prompt behaving the same way.

Three causes, compounding:

  • No per-turn signal exists. app/agent/language.py formats a setting into a sentence. Nothing anywhere computes the language of the current inbound message. "The language the customer used" is genuinely ambiguous when the model can see forty turns of them.
  • The rule is at position 0. It is the furthest possible point in the request from the message it governs, with an Urdu-heavy history and an Urdu-flavoured summary in between.
  • Your own instructions carry a standing prior. ④ contains "Many write Roman Urdu; reply in Roman Urdu when they do", which is present on every turn regardless of what this customer wrote.

One more detail worth knowing: reply_language is NULL in the database. The Behavior page shows "Match the customer" selected, but nothing was ever saved; the code falls back to match. The earlier fix moved language from prose into a setting, which changed where the sentence comes from — not whether the sentence wins.

FixDetect the script and language of the current message, cheaply and deterministically — Arabic block versus Latin, plus a Roman-Urdu heuristic — and inject it into build_turn_prompt immediately above Customer message: as a fact, not a rule: "The customer's current message is in English (Latin script). Reply in that language and that script." A stated fact about this message cannot be out-voted by history. Do the same on the voice leg, from the transcript, before TTS. And pin the rolling summary to one language so it stops voting.

E2
Correction

The callback promise is not invented. You instructed it, word for word.

Your custom_instructions contain:

"A booking on WhatsApp is a request: take the city, the tier, the branch,
the service, the preferred date and time, the name and the phone number,
then tell the customer a representative will call within a few hours to
confirm, and that a confirmation comes on WhatsApp afterwards."

Every "a rep will call you in a few hours" in the transcript is that sentence being obeyed. The model did what it was told.

That said, the guard rail around it is genuinely too narrow. GROUNDING_INSTRUCTION forbids inventing "facts, prices, policies, or availability" — four nouns. A promise about the future is none of them, so nothing stops the model extending an instructed commitment into new ones. And the same sentence instructs it to "say you'll connect them with the team", which is itself a first-person agentic claim that it then elaborates.

FixDecide which you want, then make the two agree. If a callback is real, keep the line. If it is not, delete it from ④ and replace ⑤'s noun list with a positive rule: "Never commit to anything on the business's behalf. No timelines, no callbacks, no confirmations, no promises about what any person will do. If an action is needed, say the team has been notified, and say nothing about when."

E3
High

Line one of every prompt tells the model it is an AI.

You want customers to experience the business answering, not a bot. The first sentence of ① is You are the AI customer representative for {business}. That is the single most influential line in the request and it says the opposite of the goal.

Related and visible in the transcript: the persona changes gender mid-conversation — kar sakti hun (female) in one reply, kar sakta hun and kar deta hun (male) in others. In Urdu that is not subtle. Field ② persona is empty, so nothing pins it.

FixRewrite ① as "You are the customer service team for {business}. You speak as the business, using 'we'. You never describe yourself as an AI, a bot, an assistant or a representative, and you never discuss how you work." Then put a fixed voice and gender in ②, which is currently doing nothing.

E4
High

Seven of the eight skills have never been invoked, and one page says otherwise.

qonvo_skill_invocations_total{skill="human_handoff", outcome="escalated"}         = 2
qonvo_skill_invocations_total{skill="human_handoff", outcome="already_escalated"} = 1
                                                          (nothing else, ever)

book_appointment, check_availability, capture_lead, append_to_sheet, lookup_sheet, take_order and share_payment_details have zero invocations since the counters began.

For booking, the cause is again ④. Your instructions say "Never say a time slot is free or booked. You cannot see any diary." Meanwhile the Skills page states "Check availability — reads your calendar before offering a time, so it never offers a slot you are already busy in" and "Book an appointment — Available", and Google Calendar is connected and passing its test. Two surfaces of the same product contradict each other and nothing reconciles them.

capture_lead is a separate matter. The booking exchange collected a name, a phone number, a city and a service, and captured nothing. That lead exists only inside a chat transcript.

FixSurface the conflict in the product: if instructions contradict a connected integration, say so on the Skills page rather than letting the owner discover it in a transcript. And make capture_lead fire on a completed intake regardless, because a name and a number are worth keeping whatever else the flow does.

The pattern behind all four

Every one of these comes from the same place: the tenant's free-text instructions are the most powerful input in the system, and nothing bounds, checks or reconciles them. They are 1,821 characters against a handful of hardcoded sentences, they sit in the same undifferentiated block, and they can silently disable a paid integration, override the language setting, and commit the business to a callback. That is a product design question, not a prompt-wording one: which rules are the owner's to set, and which are yours to enforce underneath them.

05

The admin console

Four pages: Tenants, Fleet Health, System Health, Usage. Everything it claims to do, it does — every lifecycle endpoint returned correctly and the tenant detail page is genuinely thorough. What it lacks is the operational furniture that turns a working console into one you can run a business from, and one control is dangerous as drawn.

Findings

A1
High

Logout sits beside Restart at identical weight, one click from unlinking a customer's number.

Every Fleet row renders four buttons in a line: Restart, Start, Stop, Logout. Same size, same styling, no grouping, no confirmation observed. Logout ends the WhatsApp pairing and requires the customer to scan a QR code again — an outage you cannot fix from your side. Compare with tenant deletion, which correctly demands the business name typed in full.

FixSeparate the destructive pair behind a menu, require confirmation naming the tenant, and make Restart the only prominent control. Then only offer the actions that make sense for the current state — Start on a stopped session is meaningful, on a working one it is noise.

A2
High

The audit log is write-only. There is no way to read it.

Admin actions and activation changes write audit_log rows. Every plausible endpoint — /api/admin/audit, /audit-log, /logs, /events — returns 404, and there is no page. The record exists and cannot be consulted without opening psql.

That matters more than it sounds. "Who suspended this tenant", "who reset that password", "when was this rep switched off" are the first three questions of any support conversation, and impersonation is audited precisely so that somebody can review it later.

FixAn endpoint and a page. Filterable by tenant and by actor, newest first. It is a table you already populate.

A3
Medium

Overview tiles go stale the moment you create a tenant.

After creating the throwaway, the table listed four tenants while the tile above it still read BUSINESSES 3. The row arrived, the counters did not, because they come from a separate fetch that the mutation does not invalidate. An operator who trusts the tile clicks Create again and makes a duplicate.

FixInvalidate the overview query on any tenant mutation.

A4
Medium

Two working endpoints have no interface, and the plan control is a two-state toggle.

PATCH …/tenants/{id} accepts trial_ends_at and it works; nothing on the page sets it. POST …/impersonate works and is properly scoped; there is no button. PUT …/subscription, the manual billing adapter, exists and has no interface, which is why the plan control is the binary toggle behind F3.

FixA date field for trial end. A "Sign in as this owner" button, prominent about what it is. And a plan picker over the catalogue, replacing the toggle.

A5
Medium

No search, no filter, no sort, no pagination anywhere in the console.

The tenants list has no input field of any kind. It renders every tenant in creation order. That is fine at three and unusable at two hundred, and two hundred is the point at which you most need it. Fleet Health has the same shape.

FixSearch by business name and owner email, a status filter, sortable columns and paging. Fleet should default to sorting failed sessions first.

A6
Low

Every admin page load makes a request that is guaranteed to fail.

GET /api/onboarding is called on admin pages and returns 403 every time, because an admin has no tenant. Harmless in itself, and it trains you to ignore 403s in the network tab.

A7
Low

Dates are American here and human everywhere else.

The tenants table reads 9/8/2026. The owner-facing product reads 5 Sept 2026. The admin console did not get the formatter.

A8
Low

The timezone hint on a tenant's page describes your device, not theirs.

Editing a tenant's configuration shows "This device is in Asia/Karachi. Use that instead." On the owner's own Business page that is a genuinely good touch. On an operator's screen, configuring somebody else's business, it is advice to apply your own timezone to their salon.

FixSuppress it in the admin context, or replace it with the tenant's WhatsApp country code.

What the console is missing entirely

  • Alerting. Fleet Health knew a customer's session was dead. Nothing told anyone. A console you have to remember to open is a dashboard, not an operations tool.
  • Per-tenant conversation access. Support is going to be asked "what did the bot say to my customer", and the only route is impersonation, which has no button.
  • Per-tenant billing. Subscription state, invoices and payment failures are invisible from the admin side; the tenant detail page covers lifecycle and configuration but not money.
  • Error and job visibility. System Health shows six provider errors and two job failures as numbers. There is no way to see which tenant, which conversation, or what the error was — the information exists only in container logs.
  • Signup and activation funnel. Three of four tenants have never connected a number. Nothing reports that, and it is the single most useful number you could have this month.
  • Anything for the owner in trouble. Resending a verification or invitation email, or clearing a stuck ingestion, all require a database.

Interface suggestions

  • Lead with what is wrong. The console opens on six equal counters. It should open on exceptions: sessions not working, tenants over a limit, failed payments, jobs that died. Everything healthy can be one line.
  • Give the tenant detail page a spine. It is currently lifecycle, then support, then the entire owner configuration form inline — a very long scroll where a 400-entry timezone select sits between "Delete tenant" and the persona picker. Tabs, or at minimum collapse the configuration behind a disclosure.
  • Colour by state. FAILED renders in the same type as WORKING. Fleet is the one screen where a colour is worth more than a word.
  • Say which environment this is. Production and staging admin consoles are identical, and the actions here are irreversible.
  • Separate reading from doing. Suspend, delete and reset password sit in the same visual register as a name field. Danger zone is right for delete; suspend and password reset deserve the same treatment.

One thing worth saying plainly

Of the twenty-odd defects in the previous teardown, most are genuinely gone, and I verified them by using the product rather than by reading the diff. What this round found instead is a different and more interesting class of problem: not missing features, but things that fail without telling anyone.
