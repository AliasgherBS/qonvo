Product & interface review / 8 September 2026

Qonvo, walked end to end.

What the product is, how the pieces actually fit together, and forty-odd things I found by driving the live site at qonvo.org as the owner of a real tenant.

01

What the product is

Qonvo puts an AI customer representative on a business's own WhatsApp number. Not a widget on a website, not a new app for the customer to install: the number the business already prints on its shopfront starts answering in seconds, at 2 AM, in whatever language the customer wrote in, and it can act rather than only reply.

The business it is sold to is a clinic, a salon, a gym, a restaurant, a property agent. The thesis is narrow and correct: for those businesses the gap between "a customer messaged" and "someone replied" is where the revenue leaks, and nobody in a five-person salon is going to run software to close it. So Qonvo is fully managed and multi-tenant from the first line, and the owner's whole relationship with it is a web dashboard.

How a message actually travels

  • 01 IngressWAHA posts the WhatsApp event to an HMAC-signed webhook, per session
  • 02 GuardDedupe, rate limit, skip the gateway's own echo, detect owner takeover
  • 03 CoalesceDebounce ~5s so a burst of three messages becomes one turn
  • 04 GatePlan state, trial quota, activation switch, business hours
  • 05 GroundRetrieve from that tenant's pgvector chunks, budgeted to ~2k tokens
  • 06 ActTool loop: check availability, book, append a row, take an order, hand off
  • 07 ReplyText or a TTS voice note, paced like a human, capped per day

Two structural decisions are worth naming because everything else rests on them. Isolation is enforced in Postgres, not in application code: three database roles, FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY on every tenant table, and the request path running as a role that cannot bypass it. And the AI providers are configuration, not dependencies: one OpenAI-compatible adapter covers OpenAI, Gemini, Groq and anything with a base URL, selectable per tenant. That is what makes "swap the model when a provider breaks" an environment variable instead of a release.

The honest position

The engineering is ahead of the interface. Across ten authenticated pages I recorded zero console errors and zero failed requests. Skeletons, empty states, toasts, a working dark theme and a four-step product tour are all real and all considered. Almost everything below is a design or an information problem, not a stability one, and several of the worst items are single-line fixes.

02

The design docs, against what shipped

Two specs govern the interface. The August overhaul rebuilt the brand and the information architecture; the September one added limits, activation and onboarding. Both largely landed. Four things did not, and one decision has quietly expired.

D1
Expired

"Pricing ships with no numbers at all" was right in August and is wrong now.

The overhaul spec froze prices out of the repository because pricing was undecided and the old placeholders were unrealistic. Since then Polar went live, three products exist at $10 / $18 / $30, and this tenant has a real paid invoice sitting on its billing page. The premise the decision rested on is gone, but both the landing page and the in-app plan picker still behave as though prices are a secret.

FixKeep prices out of the repo, which is still correct, and read them from the provider at render time. The catalogue call already exists; it just does not carry a figure.

D2
Not built

Three of the ten planned landing treatments shipped as something simpler.

"How it works" was specified as a sticky-stack with pin-and-scrub; it shipped as a plain numbered list. The voice section was specified with a waveform that animates on play; it is a static graphic with no audio behind it. The hero poster was specified as a frame near the booking confirmation; it is the video's first frame, which is a nearly empty chat window.

FixThe poster is the cheap one and the most valuable: it is the LCP element and the only thing a reduced-motion visitor ever sees. Re-export a frame from around the confirmation.

D3
Landed well

The September spec's hardest asks all shipped, including the ones that changed behaviour.

The rep now starts off and is switched on deliberately, the switch is on every page rather than buried, usage meters read from one computation shared with the admin console, the model selector is gone from the owner's view, and the trial length has a single definition that a test defends. The product tour works, is skippable, and finds its targets by attribute rather than by fragile selectors.

03

The landing page

Dark, locked to dark, ten sections, 7,614 pixels tall at desktop. The brand is applied properly: Bricolage and Manrope are both loaded, the five kit colours are exact, and Volt appears exactly once, on the closing call to action, which is what the kit asks for. The hero video plays, loops at 12.5 seconds, and is only 568 KB.

L1
Costly

Nowhere on the public site does Qonvo say what it costs.

The pricing section says "talk to us and we will size it with you" and offers a WhatsApp button. Meanwhile the product has self-serve checkout, three tiers, and has taken money. Every visitor who wants a number has to either start a trial or open a conversation with you, and most will do neither. For a $10 to $30 product this is the single largest conversion cost on the page: at that price the buyer expects to decide alone.

FixThree tiers with figures, monthly and annual, plus the trial line that is already there. The section was designed to expand into tiers in place without disturbing the rhythm around it.

L2
Costly

"Unlimited knowledge about your business" contradicts the caps that shipped last week.

Knowledge is now bounded on every plan: 50 to 400 sources, 2M to 15M characters, 50 to 500 MB of uploads. The caps are generous and defensible, and the billing page displays them honestly. The landing page still promises unlimited. That is the kind of line a customer quotes back at you.

Fix"Room for everything you know" or the actual Starter figure. The caps are a selling point when stated confidently; they only look bad when they contradict an earlier promise.

L3
Rough

A 7,600-pixel page with no navigation at all.

The header holds the logo, "Sign in" and "Start free trial", and nothing else. Pricing sits 5,133 pixels down; the FAQ at 6,013. A visitor who arrives wanting a price, or wanting the one FAQ answer that decides it for them, has no way to get there except scrolling past everything. The footer is three links.

FixThree anchors in the header: How it works, Pricing, FAQ. That is it.

L4
Rough

Three sections in a row leave the right half of a desktop viewport empty.

The 2 AM statement, the bento heading, and "Live in a day. No code." are all left-aligned text in a wide container with nothing opposite. On a laptop the page reads as a column that forgot to fill its page. The spec called for ten distinct layout families and the sections are distinct, but three of them resolve to the same silhouette.

FixGive the 2 AM section its cost on the right, as figures or as a clock. Give "Live in a day" the promised sticky visual. Or narrow the container so the emptiness reads as intent rather than as a gap.

L5
Rough

The same conversation art appears twice, 1,600 pixels apart.

Section three, "Answered in seconds. Booked automatically.", uses a still of the identical dental booking chat that the hero video just played. Same business, same messages, same voice note. It reads as a page with one asset, and it wastes the strongest slot below the fold on repetition.

FixShow a different job here: an order being taken, or a lead landing in a spreadsheet. Different vertical, different outcome, same visual language.

L6
Polish

The language marquee repeats itself inside a single viewport.

Bonjour, Olá and Merhaba each appear twice across one screen width, which makes the loop visible and reads as a rendering bug rather than as motion. The list needs to be long enough that one screen never contains a repeat.

FixTwelve to sixteen unique greetings, and lean into the market: Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi and Roman Urdu alongside the international ones.

L7
Polish

A page selling voice has no voice on it.

"Send a voice note. Get one back." is followed by a decorative static waveform. There is nothing to press. This is the differentiator against every text-only competitor and it is the one claim on the page a visitor cannot verify without signing up.

FixOne ten-second clip of the rep answering in Urdu, with the transcript beside it. It costs one audio file and it is the most persuasive object you could put on this page.

04

Sign in and sign up

Both auth pages are well built. Google SSO sits above the fold, the fields are the right ones, the copy is plain. Three things work against them.

The theme discontinuity is the loudest: the marketing site is locked dark by deliberate decision, and clicking "Start free trial" drops the visitor onto a bright cream page. The moment of highest intent is the moment the brand changes clothes.

The logo is not a link on either page, so there is no way back to the site except the browser button.

And there is no terms or privacy consent line on signup, which you will need before a merchant of record reviews the account, and want regardless.

A1
Rough

Any unknown URL becomes a login page for logged-out visitors, and a bare browser 404 for everyone else.

/anything-mistyped answers 307 to /login?callbackUrl=... when signed out. A mistyped marketing link therefore asks a stranger to sign in, and a crawler records a soft 404. Signed in, the same URL renders the unstyled Next.js default: the words "404" and "This page could not be found." on white, with no layout, no logo, and no way back.

FixExempt unmatched paths from the auth middleware, and give the app a real not-found page inside the shell with a link to the inbox.

05

Inbox

The screen the owner lives in. Two panes, filter tabs for All / Needs human / Paused, live status per conversation, and takeover that genuinely works: pressing "Take over" flips the badge to "You're replying", enables the composer, and "Resume bot" puts it back. The mechanism underneath is the good part of this product and it is correctly exposed.

I1
Costly

Customers are labelled 923009998877@c.us and 29918758989949@lid.

This is the WhatsApp internal address, shown verbatim as the conversation title, in the list and again in the transcript header. A salon owner scanning for the woman who asked about a refund cannot find her, cannot recognise a regular, and cannot tell two customers apart at a glance. The @lid form is worse: it is not even a phone number.

FixShow the WhatsApp push name when the payload carries one, and format the number as +92 300 999 8877 otherwise. Keep the raw address in a tooltip. This is the highest ratio of perceived quality to work in the whole application.

I2
Broken

The reply box starts below the fold, and the whole page scrolls to reach it.

Measured at 1440×900: the composer sits at y=884 with the viewport ending at 900. At 1366×700 it is roughly 200 pixels under the edge. The inbox is written as a fixed-height application shell, but the page root asks for h-full against a chain that only sets min-h-screen, so the height never resolves and the panes grow past the window instead of scrolling inside it. Scrolling the page does reach the composer, at the cost of pushing the rep switch off the top.

FixGive the shell a definite height and let the panes own their own overflow. One change also fixes the conversation list, which is clipped the same way.

I3
Broken

On a phone, tapping a conversation appears to do nothing.

The two panes stack, so the transcript renders underneath the full conversation list, off-screen. The tap registers, the row highlights, and nothing visible changes. To read the chat you scroll past the entire list, and there is no way back up except scrolling again. Every messaging application on earth uses list, then detail, then a back arrow, and the owner's mental model is set by WhatsApp itself.

FixBelow the large breakpoint, show one pane at a time with a back control in the transcript header.

I4
Rough

No search, no unread state, no dates.

Fine at three conversations. At three hundred, which is what a Growth tenant looks like after a month, there is no way to find a chat, no indication which ones are new since you last looked, and no date separators in a transcript where every message reads "2 days ago". The tab row would carry counts naturally.

FixA search field over name and number, an unread dot per row with a count on the All tab, and day separators in the transcript.

I5
Polish

Small things that add up in the transcript.

Every bubble carries a "Customer" or "Bot" label above it, which alignment and colour already say. The Urdu preview in the list is right-to-left inside a left-to-right row, so it truncates from the wrong end and the ellipsis lands at the start of the sentence. Every conversation gets the same generic person icon.

FixDrop the role labels, set dir="auto" on message text and previews, and give the avatar an initial from the contact name once names exist.

06

Knowledge

Drop zone, source table, and a Gaps tab. The ingestion story is complete: files, pasted text and website URLs all work and report a real status. This is the page where a rep stops being a demo, and the spec knew that.

K1
Rough

Gaps is the best idea in the product and it is a dead end.

The system already knows exactly what it failed to answer, and shows it to the owner in a clean table. Then it stops. There is no way to answer the question from here, no link to the conversation it came from, no date. The loop that would make the rep visibly improve week over week is one button away and the button is not there.

FixAn "Answer this" action per row that opens the new-entry form with the question pre-filled as the title, and marks the gap resolved on save. This is also the "it gets better over time" story the product currently cannot tell.

K2
Rough

A source is a name, a type and a date, and nothing else.

The table cannot tell you how much a source contributed, when a website was last crawled, or how to crawl it again. A URL source that went stale six weeks ago looks identical to one fetched this morning. The one "Refresh" control belongs to the list, not the source, but sitting at the top of a table of crawled websites it reads as though it re-fetches them.

FixAdd a size or chunk count and a last-updated column, a per-row re-crawl for URLs, and rename the list control so it cannot be misread.

K3
Rough

The knowledge caps are invisible on the knowledge page.

Sources used, characters stored and megabytes uploaded are all metered against the plan, and all three are shown only on the billing page. The moment an owner needs that number is while they are dragging the ninth PDF onto the drop zone.

FixOne quiet line under the drop zone: "2 of 50 sources, 7,775 of 2,000,000 characters". The computation already exists and is shared with the admin console.

K4
Polish

Dates render as 9/5/2026.

Month-first for a product whose first market is Pakistan, where that reads as 9 May. It is ambiguous for the entire calendar except twelve days a year.

Fix"5 Sep 2026" everywhere, from one formatter.

07

Behavior

Persona and tone, primary language, custom instructions with a live counter that turns amber at 80 percent, business hours, and voice mode. The counter, the placeholder guidance and the note that "every word here is read on every single reply" are all excellent: they teach the owner the cost model without saying the word token.

This page carries the most consequential bug in the application, and it is invisible from the screen itself.

The business hours payload includes a timezone. It defaults to UTC, it is written on every save, and no control anywhere in the dashboard renders it. The value exists in the client API layer and is honoured by the worker that decides whether the business is open.

So a salon in Lahore setting 9:00 to 17:00 has told the system it is open from 2 PM to 10 PM local. The rep sends the closed-for-the-day auto-reply through the entire morning rush, and answers normally at ten at night. Nothing on screen would ever explain why.

B1
Broken

Business hours are evaluated in UTC and there is no way to change that.

Confirmed three ways: the client sends timezone: "UTC" as a default, the worker reads business_hours.timezone and converts local time against it, and a search of every component finds no input bound to that field. The failure is silent, it only appears once an owner turns hours on, and the symptom is the rep refusing to talk to customers during business hours.

FixA timezone select at the top of the business hours card, defaulted from the browser rather than to UTC, with the current local time shown beside it so the setting confirms itself.

B2
Rough

The hours master switch says Off while the seven day rows look fully active.

Green toggles, editable time fields, full contrast. The only signal that none of it applies is a grey sentence above them. An owner who carefully sets Sunday closed and saves has changed nothing, and has no way to know.

FixDim and disable the rows when the master is off, or collapse them behind it entirely.

B3
Rough

A 2,000 character field in a five-line box, and one Save button for three cards.

The custom instructions field is the most important text in the tenant's configuration and it is edited through a window showing about a fifth of it. Below it, business hours and voice sit in separate cards but share a single Save at the very bottom of a 1,900 pixel page, with no indication that anything is unsaved.

FixMake the instructions box roughly twelve lines and resizable. Either give each card its own save, as the overhaul spec intended, or make the single one a sticky bar that appears when the form is dirty.

B4
Polish

Seven identical rows with no way to set them together, and a wide page used narrowly.

Setting Monday to Friday means seven repetitions of the same two fields. And every settings page confines its form to about 745 pixels inside a 1,150 pixel content area, so roughly a third of the screen is permanently empty on all of them.

Fix"Apply to weekdays" and "Copy to all days". Then either widen the cards or put the guidance text in the empty column, where it would be read rather than skipped.

08

Skills, and Business

P1
Rough

The Skills page lists no skills.

It promises "what your AI rep can do for a customer beyond answering questions" and delivers a handover phone number, a payment details box, and one sentence pointing at Integrations. The eight things the rep can actually do — check availability, book an appointment, append to a sheet, look up a sheet, take an order, share payment details, capture a lead, hand off to a human — are never named anywhere in the dashboard. The owner cannot see what they bought, cannot turn one off, and cannot tell which ones are gated behind a connection they have not made.

FixMake this a real list: one row per skill, with a plain-language description, a state (active / needs Google / needs payment details), and a switch. The registry and the gating metadata already exist on the server; this is a rendering job, and it is how the product demonstrates that it is an agent rather than a chatbot.

P2
Rough

Business is a whole navigation entry for one text field.

"Business name". That is the entire page. It had a second purpose until the model selector was correctly removed, and nothing replaced it. Meanwhile the settings that genuinely belong to the business — the timezone, the country, the handover number — are scattered across Behavior, Skills and the Google Calendar card.

FixEither fold the field into Behavior and drop the entry, or make Business the home for business facts: name, timezone, country, contact number. The second is better, and it gives the timezone from B1 an obvious place to live.

09

Integrations

The strongest piece of interaction design in the product. Per-tenant Google OAuth, the calendar provisioned for you, the sheet chosen through Google's own picker, a working test that returns a clear toast, and a disconnect that knows not to revoke the grant the other integration is still using. "Sign in once; there's nothing to copy or paste" is both a promise and, unusually, true.

N1
Broken

The calendar timezone is also UTC, so bookings land five hours off.

This is a different field from B1 — it belongs to the integration rather than to business hours — but it defaults the same way and is set the same way. A customer who books "3 PM tomorrow" gets an event at 8 PM Karachi time, on the owner's real calendar, with a confirmation message that says three o'clock. Availability checks read the same clock, so the rep will also offer slots the owner is not free in.

FixDefault from the browser at connect time rather than to UTC, and show the resolved local time next to the select. Better still, set it once on Business and have both places read it.

N2
Rough

Reconnect is the primary button on a connection that is working.

Green and filled, it is the loudest thing on the card, sitting next to a badge that says Connected. It is a recovery action and should be the quietest of the three. Test connection is what an owner actually wants, and it is styled as secondary.

FixSwap the emphasis. Reconnect becomes a text link beside Disconnect, and only turns loud when the connection reports a problem.

N3
Rough

Nothing on the card proves the integration has ever been used.

"Connected" says the token works. It does not say the rep has booked anything or written a row. The owner's real question is whether this thing is doing its job, and answering it currently means opening Google.

FixOne line per card: "Last booking 2 hours ago" / "Last row written yesterday, 14 rows this month". You already record both.

10

WhatsApp

W1
Broken

The WhatsApp page only knows how to do first-run setup.

A connected tenant sees the same empty connect form as a brand new one. There is no status, no indication of which number is linked, no QR code to re-link, no logout, no last-seen. The number is the product; this is the page that says whether the product is plugged in, and it says nothing.

This also blocks the recovery story that matters most. When a WhatsApp session drops — and web sessions do drop, on phone reboots, on WhatsApp's own logouts — the owner has no surface that tells them, and no control to fix it. Today the only place a session can be restarted is the internal admin console, which means every disconnection is a support ticket.

FixMake this a status page first and a setup form second: connected number, state, last event received, a QR block when it needs re-linking, and Restart / Log out. The endpoints for status and QR already exist on the owner API.

W2
Rough

It asks a salon owner to invent a "session name".

The label is Session name and the example is main-support-line. That is an internal identifier for a WAHA session, surfaced to a non-technical buyer as the first thing they must decide, with the button disabled until they do.

FixGenerate it. If a tenant ever needs two numbers, ask "What should we call this number?" and accept "Front desk".

11

Analytics

Y1
Broken

The only chart in the product draws nothing.

The data is there and the markup is correct: two columns exist, carrying height: 100% and height: 5% and tooltips reading "2026-09-05: 40 messages". Every one of them measures 0 pixels. The row is h-40 with items-end, which sizes each column to its content instead of stretching it, so the percentage heights resolve against zero. The empty-state path never fires, because the data is not empty — the chart just paints nothing.

FixGive the columns full height, or move the row to a grid with align-items: stretch. Then give it axis labels and a date under each bar, because at two bars across 1,100 pixels the corrected chart will still say very little.

Y2
Rough

Eight tiles of identical weight, five of them reading zero.

Messages, conversations, leads, bookings, orders, needs human, open handoffs and AI cost all get the same box, the same type and the same emphasis. Nothing says which of these matters, nothing compares to last month, and the period is fixed at 30 days with no control. A page whose job is "is this working" answers with a wall of equally loud numbers.

FixLead with the two figures the owner is buying — messages answered and bookings or leads created — at a larger size, with a change against the previous period. Demote the rest to a row of small stats, and add a range selector.

Y3
Rough

"AI cost $0.02" shows the customer your cost of goods.

It is precise, honest, and an odd thing to put in front of someone paying $10 a month. It invites exactly one question, and the answer is a margin conversation you would rather not open on the analytics page. Internally it is a vital number; the admin console is where it belongs.

FixReplace it with something the owner values — median first-response time, or hours of replying saved — and keep the cost figure in the admin fleet view where it already lives.

12

Billing

The best page in the product, and it is not close. Six meters against real entitlements, a reset date, a renewal date, a real invoice from a real payment, a card update that goes to the provider's portal, and a cancel that explains what cancelling actually does. The writing is precise where it needs to be: "Cancel any time and keep everything until then" is the sentence that stops a support email.

Z1
Costly

The plan picker has no prices either. You press Upgrade to find out what it costs.

"Growth — 5,000 messages a month, 5 team seats — Upgrade". Pressing that button is how the owner discovers the figure, on the provider's checkout page. Asking someone to start a payment flow to learn a price is the most expensive possible way to present one, and it undermines a page that is otherwise exceptionally candid.

FixFetch the figures from the provider catalogue and render them here and on the landing page from the same source. The principle that prices live with the merchant of record is right; it never required hiding them.

Z2
Rough

Voice minutes are metered above and invisible in the plan comparison.

The meters show 0 of 5 voice minutes. The plan rows describe Growth and Scale purely by messages and seats. Voice is the entitlement most likely to make someone upgrade, it is the one that costs you the most to serve, and it is missing from the exact moment the decision is made.

FixAdd voice minutes and knowledge allowance to each plan row, and highlight the one the tenant is closest to exhausting.

Z3
Polish

Two dates that look like they disagree, and empty meters that look broken.

"Messages and voice reset on Oct 1" sits directly above "Renews on October 7, 2026". Both are true — one is the calendar quota month, the other the billing anniversary — but nothing says so, and a customer reading quickly sees a bug. Separately, the meters at zero render as flat grey tracks with no fill, which reads as a disabled control rather than as unused allowance.

FixOne clause joining the dates: "Usage resets on the 1st; your plan renews on the 7th." Give zero meters a visible empty track with a faint start cap.

13

The shell, the phone, and the edges

S1
Broken

The mobile navigation bar is not fixed, so it sits at the bottom of the document.

Measured on the inbox at 390×844: the bar renders at y=1114 in an 1,181 pixel document. It is in normal flow, not pinned. On a short page you scroll to find it; on Behavior or Billing, which run to several thousand pixels, navigation is a long way past the end of the content. A bottom bar that has to be scrolled to is not a bottom bar.

Fixfixed bottom-0 with matching bottom padding on the main region. One line.

S2
Broken

Five pages have no way to be reached on a phone.

The bottom bar holds Inbox, Knowledge, Behavior, Apps and Analytics. Its own comment says everything else is "reachable from the account menu" — but the account menu contains Account, Theme and Sign out. So Billing, Team, Business, Skills and WhatsApp are unreachable on mobile except by typing the URL. Billing is where an owner goes when their rep has stopped working, and the phone is what they have in their hand.

FixReplace the fifth slot with a "More" sheet listing the rest, or add them to the account menu as the comment already assumes.

S3
Rough

The rep switch sits above the page title on every single page.

Putting it everywhere was the right call — it is the control you reach for in a hurry. But it is rendered as a full-width card above the content, so on desktop every H1 starts 90 pixels down, and on a phone it consumes 130 pixels of the first screen on all thirteen pages. Something you need occasionally has taken the position of something you need constantly.

FixMove it into the top bar as a compact switch with a state label. It stays global, stays one click, and stops taxing every page.

S4
Rough

Notifications have no badge, no link, and quote the model's reasoning back at you.

The bell shows no unread indicator, so there is nothing to prompt a click. Opening it reveals: "A customer needs a human. Customer requests a refund for a colour service that was ruined last week. This is a complaint/refund scenario, which requires a human handoff." The last sentence is the model explaining itself to the system. The notification does not link to the conversation, which is the only thing you would want to do with it.

FixA dot with a count on the bell, the customer's name in the title, the reason trimmed to its first sentence, and the whole row linking to the chat.

S5
Polish

Three small dead spots.

"Show the setup checklist" in the help menu does nothing once setup is complete, with no feedback either way. The Account page presents email and role as disabled input boxes, which read as a broken form rather than as facts. And Team's top-right primary action is "Export data", which is an account-level concern wearing the costume of a page action, while the seat count that belongs next to the invite form lives only on Billing.

FixHave the checklist item say "You're all set" when there is nothing left. Render read-only values as text with a label. Move Export to Account, and put "1 of 2 seats used" beside the invite button.

S6
Working well

Things I went looking to criticise and could not.

The product tour runs four steps over the real interface, rings its target, is escapable, and remembers per browser so an invited teammate gets their own. Dark mode is complete and arguably better looking than light, and it matches the marketing site. Toasts are clear and correctly placed. Skeletons match the shape of what loads into them. Empty states say what to do rather than "no data". The knowledge character counter turns amber at exactly the right moment. And across ten pages at four widths, the browser console stayed silent.

14

What I would do, in order

Ordered by damage prevented per hour spent, not by size. The first four are all small.

  1. Give the timezone a control, and default it from the browser

    Two fields, both silently UTC. One makes the rep refuse customers during business hours; the other books appointments five hours from when the customer was told. Both fail invisibly, and the owner's conclusion will be that the product does not work.

    B1 · N1

  2. Fix the analytics chart

    A CSS height that resolves against zero. The one visual on the page that argues the product is doing something currently argues the opposite.

    Y1

  3. Pin the mobile navigation and give it the missing pages

    Two changes, both small, and they turn the phone experience from unusable into usable.

    S1 · S2

  4. Show prices, in the app and on the site

    Three tiers exist, checkout works, a payment has cleared. Nothing is gained by making a buyer press Upgrade to learn a figure, and the "unlimited knowledge" line has to go with it.

    L1 · L2 · Z1

  5. Name the customers in the inbox
